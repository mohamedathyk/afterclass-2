CUISINE

Maldivian Cuisine: A Taste of the Islands Maldivian cuisine is deeply rooted in the geography and culture of the Maldives — a tropical paradise in the Indian Ocean. Its food reflects the island nation's abundant marine life, coconut palms, and historical trade connections with India, Sri Lanka, and the Middle East.

Key Ingredients Fish : Especially tuna, like skipjack (Katsuo), is a staple. Fish is eaten fresh, smoked, dried, or cured. Coconut: Used in many forms: grated, milk, oil, or as a flavor enhancer. Starches:Such as rice, sweet potatoes, cassava, and breadfruit. Spices:Mild but flavorful, with influences from Indian and Sri Lankan cuisine.

Popular Dishes Mas Huni: A beloved Maldivian breakfast dish made with shredded smoked tuna, coconut, onions, and chili, typically served with roshi (flatbread) and tea. Garudhiya:A clear fish broth with chunks of tuna, served with rice, lime, chili, and onions. It’s comforting and aromatic. Fihunu Mas: Grilled, spicy fish often marinated in a blend of Maldivian chili paste and coconut. Rihaakuru :A thick, brown, and intensely flavored fish paste made by slow-boiling tuna broth. It's often eaten with rice or roshi. Gulha:Deep-fried pastry balls stuffed with tuna, coconut, and spices — a popular snack. Boshi Mashuni:A salad-like mix of banana flower, coconut, and spices, sometimes with tuna added.

Drinks & Sweets Ruku Raa: A traditional fermented toddy from palm trees (mildly alcoholic). Sai : Black tea, often served sweetened. Bondibaiy:A sticky sweet rice pudding with coconut milk and sugar, often made during festivals or special occasions.

Cultural Notes Meals are typically simple, centered around rice and fish. Coconut and chili are essential flavorings. Many dishes are designed to be shared, reflecting a communal eating culture.
